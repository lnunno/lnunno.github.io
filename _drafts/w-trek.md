46.83 miles total
8,928 feet elevation gain total

## Day 1: Welcome Center to Base Torres to Chileno

Base Torres we saw about half of the towers, top half was covered in clouds but still a lot of good sights along the way. The hike to Chileno from the Welcome Center was pretty pleasant, the climb to the Base Torres was a lot more challenging, around 2k feet of elevation gain in a rocky valley with not a lot to see until the top.

## Day 2: Chileno to Frances

Probably our favorite day, great weather and incredible views the whole way. On one side views of Paine Grande glacier and progressively got closer to it and the other side beautiful bright turquoise lake views and rolling green hills that reminded me of Northern California mountains or Ireland/Scotland.

We got to walk along the shore of the lake close to the end of the trail on beautiful tide polished stones that sounded like glass chimes as you walked over them.

## Day 3: Frances to Paine Grande

The climb up Frances Valley was pretty challenging. Relatively humble distance up the valley and deceptive signage in terms of distance but the views are the best in the whole W Trek. We got a close up view of Paine Grande and saw bits of ice break off multiple times in a huge roar that sounded like thunder. Even at the beginning of the trail of the valley you can see this massive glacier and right away we heard it's huge crash of ice avalanching down.

When we finished in the valley there's another 5 miles to go and it was a bit of a slog.

## Day 4: Paine Grande to Grey

We got rain blasted shortly into the initial climb, there was also intense wind, both sustained and gusts. It felt like you were going through a full blast car wash. Luckily our gear held up well but the reputation of the Patagonia weather showed true here, definitely the most intense weather I've ever hiked through and there's no turning back here so you have to brace yourself, tighten up your rain flaps, and push forward.

## Day 5: Refugio Grey to Suspension Bridge and Mirador Grey Glacier

The Grey Glacier honestly looks just ok from the refugio, especially after all the sights we've seen on the trip, however going to the overlook point makes it easier to appreciate the sheer size of it and see it in greater detail, which makes it more impressive.

We saw some of the ice break off while we were viewing it from the overlook, either it happens more than I expected or we got pretty lucky with our glacier timings.
